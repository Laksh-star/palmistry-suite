# Hastasañjīvana — rights and source review (I2)

Prepared 9 November 2026 for the owner. This is a source-provenance analysis, not legal advice. The open India question should go to Indian counsel before any commercial release.

## 1. The work and its layers
The 1929/30 print contains four layers. Each has a different rights position.

| Layer | Who and when | United States | India | Use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Root text (mūla), Sanskrit | Meghavijaya Gaṇi, active 1674–1695 (Zysk 2025 p. 241). Some catalogues treat the root as an earlier anonymous Jain text with Meghavijaya as commentator. | Public domain | Public domain | **Usable** |
| *Sāmudrika-laharī* commentary, Sanskrit | Attributed to Meghavijaya in manuscript colophons (Mitra; Peterson, Ulwar Catalogue 1892; Gode dates it 1680–1700) | Public domain | Public domain | **Usable** |
| Editing and preface | Pratāp Muni (ed.), Muni Śrī Mohanlālajī Jaina Granthamālā No. 8, Indore, Vīra Saṃvat 2456 = 1929/30 CE (Zysk dates it 1929) | Public domain: published before 1931; the 95-year term ended by 1 January 2026 | **Unknown.** India protects works for the author's life plus 60 years, and Pratāp Muni's death date was not found. Indian courts also give mere editing of an old text thin protection (Eastern Book Co. v. D. B. Modak, 2008). | Use only for locating passages until cleared. Do not reproduce the preface or the editor's notes. |
| Abridged translation | Printed alongside the edition. The OCR shows it is **Gujarati**, not Hindi as the catalogue says. | Public domain (same date) | **Unknown** (same reason) | Hold back. Not needed if we translate afresh. |

The Digital Library of India scan (archive.org `in.ernet.dli.2015.407690`) adds no new rights. Its catalogue date of "1959" is wrong: the title page is the Granthamālā No. 8 edition. A second scan, `in.ernet.dli.2015.406217` (catalogued 1946), appears to be another copy of the same edition and has not yet been compared.

## 3. Recommendation
1. **Change I2 from "pending" to "core (Sanskrit layers only)".** That means the root text and the *Sāmudrika-laharī*, cited to the 1929/30 print by page and verse.
2. **Hold back Pratāp Muni's preface and the Gujarati abridgement** until his dates are found, or treat them as out of scope permanently.
3. **Translate from the Sanskrit afresh, with a human Sanskrit reviewer**, because the OCR is too noisy to quote. Where Zysk 2025 already translates a passage, use his translation under CC BY-SA and mark that content as BY-SA.
4. **Licence for the content repository:** the simplest consistent choice is CC BY-SA 4.0 for content/rules and content/sources, kept separate from the code licence.

## 4. Content finding: BS 68.44 resolved into a documented conflict
- **Iyer (1884):** "three lines issuing from the wrist reach the middle of the palm".
- **Zysk (2025, p. 257 comment):** reads BS 67.44ab and Garuḍa Purāṇa 1.65.47 as "three lines on the wrist", meaning the bracelet lines.
- ***Hastasañjīvana* vv. 39–41 (about p. 168):** reads the wrist bracelets (*yavamālā*) with three = king, two = minister, one = wealth, and backs this with a Prakrit verse, *maṇibandhe jassa tinni rehāu*.

The Sanskrit (*tisro rekhā maṇibandhanotthitāḥ karatalopagāḥ*) supports either reading. Both readings are now recorded under R-I-002, and neither may be evaluated until the reviewer rules. A new rule, R-I-004, covers the bracelets on their own. It needs the wrist in frame, so the capture guidance must change.

## 5. Naming trap for the crosswalk
In the Indian system, *āyurekhā* ("life line") is the line nearest the fingers, the one Western books call the heart line (Zysk 2025, p. 244). The user interface must never render *āyurekhā* as "life line" in a Western sense. The crosswalk marks these two as the same crease with different meanings, not as equivalents.
