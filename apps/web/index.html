From the archive

Famous hands, read the same way

Ink impressions that Cheiro took and published in 1894–1916, now in the public domain. Tap a hand to see how both traditions read it.

Prints from Cheiro, Language of the Hand (1897; Wellcome Collection scan, Public Domain Mark) and Palmistry for All (1916; Project Gutenberg). An ink impression is a mirror image of the hand, and old prints are often faint, so features we couldn't see clearly are left unread. These are traditional interpretations of a print, not statements about the person's life.

Please note. Palmistry Suite presents traditional interpretations from historical texts, for reflection and entertainment. Palmistry is not scientifically validated; nothing here is a prediction, a diagnosis, or advice about health, relationships, money or any decision. We do not read health, lifespan, marriage, children or the timing of events. Interpretations are paraphrased from public-domain sources (Cheiro 1916; Benham 1900; Heron-Allen 1885; Varāhamihira, Bṛhatsaṃhitā, tr. Iyer 1884; Meghavijaya, Hastasañjīvana) and from K. G. Zysk, “Early Indian Palmistry” (2025, CC BY-SA 4.0). Some Indian interpretations are awaiting scholarly review of the Sanskrit.
